05 / Mathematical foundations

Why the patterns appear.

Assume independent, identically distributed increments Xᵢ with E[Xᵢ] = μ and Var(Xᵢ) = σ².

01 / RAW SUM

Independent increments add their variances. As n increases, the absolute spread grows, even while relative fluctuations can shrink.

The Weak Law of Large Numbers guarantees convergence in probability toward μ. For any ε > 0, P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) → 0.

03 / CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM

Root-n normalization

Sn / √n
Expected valueμ√n
Varianceσ² (constant)

For the symmetric Rademacher case, μ = 0 and σ² = 1, giving Sₙ/√n ⇒ N(0,1) as n grows.

Bernoulli increments · Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}

Each system contributes 1 with probability p, and 0 otherwise. Here μ = p and σ² = p(1 − p). The raw normalized score Sₙ/√n has mean p√n and therefore does not converge to N(0,1) for fixed p > 0. Instead, the standardized, centered quantity (Sₙ − np) / √(np(1 − p)) converges to N(0,1) when 0 < p < 1.

Rademacher increments · Xᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}

Each system contributes +1 or −1 with equal probability 1/2. Consequently μ = 0 and σ² = 1. The uncentered expression Sₙ / √n is already centered and variance-normalized: the CLT applies directly. A negative total represents a negative score, not a negative number of successful breaches.

07 / Cybersecurity interpretation

What do n and m actually change?

The trajectories are a simplified mathematical analogy for penetration scoring, not a model of real-world exploit success rates.

↗

Increasing n: more systems

Each attacker visits more systems. Under Bernoulli, expected total success score rises as np. Under Rademacher, the expected total remains zero, while its standard deviation grows as √n. In either case, the average becomes more stable.

≋

Increasing m: more attackers

More independent attackers mean more sample paths in the experiment. Histograms and estimated probabilities become more reliable, but m does not change the theoretical distribution of a single attacker's final score.

◎

What the model assumes

Every increment is independent and identically distributed. Actual attack attempts may share vulnerabilities, dependencies, learning effects, defenses, and adaptive strategies. The simulation illustrates probability laws under idealized assumptions.

Suggested experiment

Choose Rademacher and m = 3,000. Raise n from 20 to 2,000: the average becomes concentrated near 0, and the normalized distribution approaches a bell curve. Switch to Bernoulli and notice that Sₙ/√n shifts to the right rather than converging to a standard normal.
